A tuner dial for the MUSIC submenu

Three controls in the bottom chrome, one job each: the nav disc opens the arc, the ball rolls through the five MUSIC destinations, and the transport button owns playback through four gestures. Pull the transport up and the full player carries the same switcher as an analog instrument. The selection is printed at 28px throughout — a little over three times the size of the 9px mono labels the arc nav uses for the same job today.

The transport button

Four outcomes on one control, and each direction means exactly one thing. Nothing fires until you let go, and the armed action is named in a chip above your thumb while you drag — so a wrong direction can be walked back to centre and cancelled rather than undone. The three drag directions are drawn faintly around the button at rest, because a gesture set nobody can see is a gesture set nobody uses.

GestureResult
TapPlay / pause
Drag rightNext track
Drag leftPrevious track
Drag upOpen the full player

The analog switcher

In the middle of the full player, the same five destinations become an instrument: a milled bezel, an engraved arc, and a needle that swings to the one you picked. Turn the hub, or focus it and use the arrow keys.

It reads and writes the same state as the ball in the chrome — one index, two bodies — so the two switchers can never disagree, and the chrome stays visible under the player rather than being covered by it.

Keyboard: focus the ball or the hub, then ← →, or Home / End.

Why this might help the readability problem

The arc nav labels every destination at once, which forces each label small. Showing one at a time lets that one be large — 28px here, against 9px today — and gives colour as a second channel alongside the word, which helps when the word itself is still hard to read. All five hues clear AA on the navy ground (5.71–11.79:1), so the label can safely take the selection's colour.

Every control here is at or over the 44px touch floor in mobile-fit.css: the ball is 58px, both discs 52px, the analog hub 74px.

What I would want to test before building it

Gestures on one button is still the risk, even now that each direction means one thing. The armed-action chip is what makes it honest — you see the outcome named before you commit — but whether that survives a real thumb on a real phone is a question for a device, not a mockup. Worth noting there is no longer any way to restart the current track from the chrome; if that turns out to matter, it belongs in the full player rather than as a second meaning for a direction that already has one.

Two switchers for one setting is also a bet. They share state here so they cannot drift, but a member still has to learn that the ball and the dial are the same control wearing different clothes.
